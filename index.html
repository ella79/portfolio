About me

I have spent fifteen years in software quality assurance, and the last six of them building automation instead of running tests by hand. Today I own the test automation strategy for safety critical embedded products at BTC Embedded Systems, where a regression suite that lies to you is worse than no suite at all.

Most of my work starts the same way. A suite is slow, flaky, or missing an entire layer, and someone has to decide what to rebuild and in what order. I led the migration of an end to end suite from Cypress to Playwright and TypeScript, then built a visual regression framework from scratch and scaled both across four consecutive automotive and HIL product lines. Flaky failures and escaped bugs dropped by 30 to 40 percent, and a full regression run got about 35 percent shorter.

Over the past year I have been folding AI tooling into that work in production, not as a side experiment. I replaced the team's GitHub Copilot workflow with Claude Code, Playwright Agents, Skills and MCP, and Figma MCP across two projects, which cut framework maintenance and test authoring time by 27 percent. I am certified by Anthropic on Claude Code, Agent Skills, Subagents and the Model Context Protocol, and I write my own agents and skills in TypeScript.

How I approach QA

Four working principles I have arrived at after fifteen years, and what each one looks like in practice.

A suite nobody trusts is worse than no suite

A flaky test is a defect in the test code, not background noise to be retried away. When I rebuilt the end to end suite in Playwright I treated stability as the acceptance criterion, which is how flaky failures and escaped bugs came down by 30 to 40 percent.

Testing belongs inside the pipeline, not after it

Quality gates that block a failing build are worth more than a report nobody opens. I put GraphQL API coverage next to the UI suites inside CI/CD, which moved roughly 20 percent more backend defects to before the release instead of after it.

Automate the maintenance, not only the tests

Most automation effort is spent keeping a framework alive rather than writing new coverage. That is where agentic tooling actually pays: Claude Code, Playwright MCP and agents I write myself took 27 percent off maintenance and authoring time across two projects.

Own the strategy, not just the tickets

Deciding what to test, in which layer, and what to stop testing is the part that needs a senior engineer. I have owned test strategy independently in Agile teams and kept on time delivery across every quarterly release for four consecutive years.
